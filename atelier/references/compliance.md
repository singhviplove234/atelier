# Legal & compliance pre-launch checklist — don't ship an app that creates legal exposure

Standing requirement: an AI-built app can be correct, fast and secure and still get its owner sued — over copied code, data privacy, accessibility or missing terms. The builder applies this checklist on every build and `/pf-harden` re-checks it on the live site. This is **practical risk reduction, not legal advice**: it flags the common gaps and, for anything real-money or client-facing, says plainly when a human lawyer should sign off.

## A. What the builder does on every build
1. **Dependency licences.** Run a licence check on `package.json` (`npx license-checker --summary` or `npx licensee`). Flag any dependency that is **not** permissive (MIT/BSD/Apache/ISC) — especially GPL/AGPL/SSPL in a proprietary client app — in `notes:` as `licence risk: <pkg> (<licence>)`. Never remove a dependency silently; list it for a decision.
2. **No copied/plagiarised code or assets.** Use only the project's own code, the chosen libraries, and the open fonts/icons from `design-standard.md`. Never paste code, images, logos, fonts or copy from an unknown source into the repo. If the Brief supplies third-party content, record its source in the README and assume it needs the owner's permission.
3. **Privacy policy.** If the app collects any personal data (sign-up, contact form, analytics, payments), generate a `/privacy` page from the Brief's `Entity:` facts stating what is collected, why, how long it's kept, and how to request deletion. Link it in the footer. If nothing personal is collected, skip it and note that.
4. **Cookie / tracking consent.** Only essential cookies before consent; any analytics or marketing script loads **after** an explicit opt-in (ties into the performance + security contracts). A short consent banner when non-essential tracking exists; none needed when it doesn't. Separately, **any form that collects personal data** (sign-up, contact, newsletter, lead form) carries its own explicit, unticked consent checkbox stating what the data is used for — form consent is distinct from cookie consent and both apply when both are present.
5. **Terms of service.** For any app with accounts, payments or user-generated content, generate a `/terms` page (plain-language starter) and link it. Mark it in `notes:` as "starter terms — have a lawyer review before real customers".
6. **Data-subject rights (access, correct, delete).** Account-holding apps expose a working "delete my account/data" path (ties into `security.md` — Supabase `auth.admin.deleteUser` + cascading deletes) **and** a way for the user to **see the personal data held on them and correct it** (export/view + edit), not deletion alone — access and rectification are separate rights from erasure. Confirm each exists and works.
7. **Accessibility.** Already enforced by the design + SEO contracts (WCAG AA, alt text, focus states) — this checklist just confirms it, since accessibility is also a legal exposure (ADA-style claims) for public sites.
8. **Age / sensitive data.** If the Brief implies users under 13, health, or financial data, do **not** guess the rules: stop and flag `needs legal review: <reason>` (COPPA / HIPAA / PCI-style obligations are out of scope for the factory).
9. **Third-party API & service terms (not just licences).** For every external API or SaaS the app calls (payments, email, maps, AI models, auth), check the provider's **terms of use**, not only an open-source licence: commercial-use allowed, data-sharing/retention limits, rate/branding requirements, whether sending user or client data to it is permitted. List each in `notes:` as `api: <name> — <terms OK / needs review: reason>`. This matters most for data-sensitive apps that send client or financial data to a third party.
10. **Attribution / NOTICES.** Where a dependency or asset licence requires credit (many MIT/BSD/Apache packages, and any Creative-Commons or purchased asset), ship the required notices — generate a `/licenses` page or a `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.txt` from the licence texts (`npx license-checker --out`), and credit CC/stock assets where their terms say. Using a permissive package is fine; silently dropping its required attribution is the exposure.
11. **AI-generated code & asset provenance.** Because the factory builds with AI, flag the IP risk directly: do not ship AI-generated code that reproduces a recognisable copyrighted snippet, and treat AI-generated images/copy as needing the same originality/licence check as any other asset. Note in `notes:` that the output is AI-assisted and was reviewed for copied material (ties into A2).

## B. How it is checked (build step 6; a `compliance` lens in `/pf-harden`)
Reuse existing tooling, no new scanner:
- Licence check clean or every non-permissive dependency listed in `notes:`; required attribution shipped (a `/licenses` page or `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.txt` present when any dependency/asset licence requires credit).
- `/privacy`, `/terms` (when required by A3/A5) return 200 and are linked in the footer; consent banner present when non-essential tracking exists; every personal-data form has its own unticked consent checkbox.
- Data-subject paths present for account apps: delete **and** access/correct (the `security.md` section-B/account test covers delete; confirm the view/edit path too).
- Each external API/service listed in `notes:` with its terms-of-use status; AI-assisted output noted as reviewed for copied material.
- A short compliance table in `notes:`: item | required? | present? | needs-human? — with a one-line "what a lawyer should still review" when the app handles payments, minors, health or multi-tenant client data.

Never: give legal advice, assert the app is "compliant" or "legal", or quietly add legal text as if a lawyer wrote it. The factory reduces obvious risk and flags the rest for a human; the final call on legal sign-off is always the owner's (and their lawyer's).
